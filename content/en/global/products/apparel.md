## Why Source Apparel from Turkey

Apparel is a fast-moving category where timing, fit and consistent quality decide success. Turkey combines nearby fabric supply, experienced garment factories and short routes to Europe, which suits both seasonal collections and quick re-orders.

- **Short distance to Europe.** Turkey borders Europe and the Middle East. Many apparel orders travel by road, which can shorten delivery compared with sea routes from distant origins and supports fast replenishment.
- **The textile chain is close.** Yarn, fabric, dyeing, printing and garment making are available in the same country. This helps you source a full collection and keep fabric and garment quality inside one supply chain.
- **Flexible small and medium runs.** Many Turkish factories accept smaller runs and quick changes between models. That suits new brands, seasonal ranges and re-orders. Minimum quantities still depend on the fabric and the factory.
- **Customs union with the EU.** Industrial goods made in Turkey can enter the European Union under the EU-Turkey customs union, subject to rules of origin. Textile products are part of this arrangement. Please check the details for your product.

## Working with apparel manufacturers in Turkey: Step by Step

1. **Brief and specification.** You share garment type, target market, quantities, fabric preferences and target price. A tech pack or a reference sample speeds up everything. If you have no tech pack, we help you define measurements and trims.
2. **Factory matching and verification.** We shortlist factories that already make similar garments. We check registration, export history, capacity and relevant certificates before you see any price.
3. **Sampling and approval.** The factory makes a proto and a fit sample. We compare measurements, fabric and workmanship with your specification and record each change. The approved sample becomes the reference for the order.
4. **Production with checks.** Fabric is tested and cut, then sewn, washed and finished. We plan a mid-production check so that problems appear while there is still time to correct them.
5. **Final inspection and shipping.** Before packing is closed, we inspect against your approved sample using AQL sampling. After a pass, we coordinate freight and export documents.

### Typical Order Terms

Minimum quantities for apparel depend on the fabric. Orders in stock fabric are usually more flexible than orders with custom yarn, dyeing or prints. Factories quote on a clear basis such as EXW or FOB, and we ask them to list fabric, trims, labels and packing as separate items so you can compare offers. Payment terms are agreed in writing, normally with a deposit at order and the balance linked to inspection and shipment. Lead time depends on fabric availability and the season, so we ask for a dated production plan before you confirm the order.

### Packing and Shipping Notes

Garments ship folded in cartons, or hanging on rails in boxes when the style needs it. Hanging transport costs more but keeps pieces smooth. We check carton count, weight and marking against the packing list, then arrange road, sea or air freight according to your deadline and budget.

## Quality Control and Certification

Apparel quality has three layers: product, safety and social compliance.

- **Product quality:** measurements within tolerance, stitching strength, colour match, wash results and finishing, checked against the approved sample.
- **Chemical and safety compliance:** buyers often ask for an OEKO-TEX Standard 100 certificate or equivalent test reports. For the EU, restricted substance rules such as REACH apply to textile products.
- **Social compliance:** many retailers require a social audit, for example amfori BSCI or a similar scheme. Ask the factory for its current report.

We carry out [factory inspection](/en/services/inspection) at the factory and send a photographed, measured report within 48 hours of the visit. We recommend a pre-shipment inspection for every order, and a mid-production check for large or first orders.

### What We Check at Inspection

- Measurements against the size chart on finished garments
- Stitching, seams, buttonholes, zippers and trims
- Fabric, colour and print match to the approved sample
- Labels, care symbols, fibre content and country of origin marking
- Folding, polybags, hangtags and carton marking
- Cartons opened at random for AQL sampling, with photographs

### Common Risks and How We Manage Them

| Risk | How we manage it |
| :--- | :--- |
| Mass production differs from the sample | Approve a signed reference sample and inspect against it, including a mid-production check. |
| Size and fit problems | Agree a measurement table with tolerances and check measurements on finished garments, also after washing. |

| Chemical or labelling non-compliance | Ask for test reports and label content in advance, and check labels at inspection. |
