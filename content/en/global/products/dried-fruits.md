## Why Source Dried Fruits from Turkey

Dried fruit is bought on grade, cleanliness and safety. Turkish producers process figs, apricots and raisins for export markets and are used to grading rules, laboratory tests and retail packaging for European and regional buyers.

- **Established fruit regions.** Dried figs, apricots and grapes are grown and processed in regions that have worked with export buyers for many years. Producers know international specifications.
- **Grades for different uses.** Fruit is sorted by size, colour and quality for retail, bakery, snack or ingredient use. You can specify the grade that matches your price and use.
- **Bulk and consumer packs.** Producers offer bulk cartons for industry and small packs for retail. Private label packing is common.
- **Near the European market.** Short transport routes help keep delivery time reasonable for a product with a limited shelf life.

## Working with dried fruit manufacturers in Turkey: Step by Step

1. **Define fruit, grade and pack.** State the fruit, size and colour grade, moisture level, treatment such as sulphur dioxide for apricots, and the pack type: bulk carton or retail pack.
2. **Producer matching.** We shortlist processors and verify registration, certificates, storage conditions and export history.
3. **Samples and laboratory tests.** We request samples and organise tests for moisture, residues and mycotoxins where relevant to the fruit and your market.
4. **Contract and production.** Grade, quantity tolerance, packing, labelling and delivery date are fixed in writing. We check the crop year and storage conditions.
5. **Inspection and shipping.** We check weight, pack integrity, labels, batch numbers and visible defects. Then we arrange transport and export documents.

### Typical Order Terms

Dried fruit prices depend on crop size, grade, packaging and order size, and they change each season. Contracts name the grade, moisture and treatment limits, packing, the crop year and the tolerance on quantity. Retail packs add label and packaging costs, and artwork must be approved before printing. We ask suppliers to quote on a clear basis such as EXW or FOB and to state what is included. Payment terms are agreed in writing, often with a deposit and a balance against documents, and laboratory tests are agreed up front, including who pays.

### Packing and Shipping Notes

Dried fruit absorbs moisture and odours, and it suffers in heat. Cartons with liners, dry pallets and clean containers help. We check packing and labels before loading and plan transport to avoid long periods in hot conditions.

## Quality Control and Certification

Dried fruit is a natural product, so quality control focuses on safety and consistency.

- **Grading:** size, colour, defects and foreign matter, compared with the agreed grade.
- **Moisture and treatment:** moisture level and any preservative treatment must match the specification and your market rules.
- **Contaminants:** buyers in many markets test for pesticide residues and mycotoxins such as aflatoxin. Strict limits and controls apply in the EU, so test before you ship.
- **Food safety systems:** look for certificates such as BRCGS, IFS, FSSC 22000 or ISO 22000, and organic certification when required.

We arrange [factory inspection](/en/services/inspection), sampling for laboratory tests and loading supervision. The inspection report follows within 48 hours of the visit.

### What We Check at Inspection

- Fruit size, colour and grade against the specification
- Moisture, foreign matter, damaged fruit and signs of infestation
- Samples drawn for laboratory tests, with sealed retained samples
- Pack type, liner, weight and labelling
- Batch numbers, crop year and best-before dates
- Pallet condition, cartons and loading conditions

### Common Risks and How We Manage Them

| Risk | How we manage it |
| :--- | :--- |
| Aflatoxin or residue above market limits | Test representative samples before shipment and source from producers with documented controls. |
| Moisture too high, causing mould | Specify moisture limits, check them at inspection and require suitable packaging. |
| Grade differs from the sample | Seal a reference sample and compare the shipped lot with it. |
| Infestation or foreign matter | Check storage conditions, inspect packing and look for pests before loading. |
| Heat damage during transport | Plan transport away from heat and avoid long waits in hot conditions. |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which dried fruits can you source from Turkey?
Common products are figs, apricots and raisins. Other dried fruits and fruit mixes may also be available. Send your product list and we check availability.

### What affects the price of dried fruit?
Crop size, grade, packaging, order size and the market all affect price. Your quotation shows the grade and conditions the price refers to.

### How do you manage aflatoxin risk?
We recommend laboratory tests on representative samples, sourcing from producers with strong controls and testing before shipment. Requirements differ by destination market.

### Can I buy organic dried fruit?
Some producers hold organic certification. We ask for the current certificate and check that it covers the product and the lot you plan to buy.

### What packaging options exist?
Bulk cartons with liners are common for industry, while retail packs suit shelf sales. Private label packing is available with your approved artwork.

### How is it shipped?
Cartons ship on pallets in containers or trucks. Protect the fruit from heat and humidity during transport. See [freight logistics](/en/services/logistics).

### Which dried fruits are most common to order?
Dried figs, apricots and raisins are the most common. Other products and mixes may also be available, so send your list.

### How should dried fruit be stored on arrival?
In a cool, dry, clean place away from strong odours. Check moisture and packaging condition when goods arrive.

### Can I buy sulphur-free apricots?
Some producers offer unsulphured apricots. They look darker and may need special storage. State your requirement in the specification.
