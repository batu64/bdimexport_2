# Importing from Türkiye to the USA: Tariffs, Import Duties and China Section 301 Comparison

For US procurement directors, retail brands, and hospitality developers evaluating **how to import from Turkey to the USA**, the very first financial question is how the **tariff on Turkey** compares against Chinese origin duties. While Türkiye and the United States do not have a bilateral Free Trade Agreement, Turkish-origin industrial and consumer goods are **exempt from US Section 301 China punitive tariffs**, creating substantial landed-cost savings across textiles, furniture, natural stone, ceramics, and metal fabrication.

## 1. How Import Duty from Türkiye to the USA Is Calculated

When clearing commercial cargo through US Customs and Border Protection (CBP), total import charges consist of four distinct components based on your 10-digit **Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS)** code:

| US Customs Charge | How CBP Calculates It | Practical Notes for Turkish Shipments |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. General (MFN / Column 1) Duty** | Percentage applied to FOB / Transaction Value (unlike the EU, the US assesses duty on FOB, not CIF) | Varies by HTS code: e.g., ~0%–4.5% on many machinery parts, ~0%–6.5% on furniture/stone, ~8%–16%+ on apparel/home textiles |
| **2. Trade Remedy / Baseline Reciprocal Tariffs** | Product- and origin-specific proclamations (such as Section 232 on steel/aluminum derivatives or baseline reciprocal schedules) | Always verify the exact 10-digit HTS chapter 99 subheading prior to purchase order issuance; zero China Section 301 surcharge applies to genuine Turkish origin |
| **3. Merchandise Processing Fee (MPF)** | **0.3464%** of entered merchandise value for formal ocean/air entries (subject to annual CBP min/max caps) | Applies to all non-FTA origins entering the United States |
| **4. Harbor Maintenance Fee (HMF)** | **0.125%** of cargo value on ocean freight arriving at US seaports (e.g., NY/NJ, Savannah, Houston, LA/Long Beach) | Does not apply to air freight |

*Note: Türkiye was removed from the US Generalized System of Preferences (GSP) in May 2019, so importers should calculate standard Column 1 MFN rates rather than legacy GSP duty-free assumptions.*

## 3. Mandatory US Import Compliance & CTPAT Container Security

Before wiring your deposit or shipping FCL/LCL cargo to the United States, ensure your Turkish manufacturer and logistics chain cover:

1. **Importer Security Filing (ISF "10+2"):** Must be transmitted electronically to CBP at least **24 hours before vessel loading** at the Turkish port to avoid $5,000+ liquidated damages penalties.
2. **Substantial Transformation & Country of Origin Marking:** Goods must be legibly marked *"Made in Türkiye"* (19 U.S.C. § 1304) and undergo genuine manufacturing transformation in Türkiye — transshipping Chinese goods through Türkiye without substantial transformation is illegal customs evasion.
3. **ISPM-15 Wood Packaging & CTPAT Seal Integrity:** All wooden pallets/crates must bear valid IPPC heat-treatment stamps, and every FCL container should undergo [pre-shipment AQL inspection and ISO 17712 high-security bolt-seal verification](/en/us/pre-shipment-and-container-loading-inspection).
