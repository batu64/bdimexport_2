### Calculation Notes

The total adds the product groups that carry no "of which" label. Each change is calculated as the 2025 value minus the 2023 value, divided by the 2023 value. Rows marked "of which" are shown for information. Their codes are already part of another row, so they are not added again.

### What the Codes Include

- **Apparel & Ready-to-Wear.** Chapters 61 and 62 cover garments and clothing accessories in all fibres, for men, women and children. They exclude footwear, leather garments and the fabrics used to make clothing, so the total is a broad indicator of the apparel sector rather than of one garment type.
- **Home Textiles, Bedding & Towels.** Headings 6301 to 6304 cover blankets, bed, table, toilet and kitchen linen (including towels), curtains and other furnishing articles. They exclude carpets, mattresses and fabrics sold by the metre, so the figure describes made-up home textiles only.
- **Technical & Nonwoven Fabrics.** Heading 5603 covers nonwovens and heading 5407 covers woven fabrics of synthetic filament yarn, which include fabrics for clothing as well as technical uses. Coated, laminated and many other technical textiles are reported under other headings, so the total does not describe all technical fabrics.
- **Denim & Jeans.** These two codes cover men's and women's cotton trousers, which include jeans but also other cotton trousers. They exclude denim fabric, denim jackets, shorts and jeans made from other fibres, so the figure is only a proxy for denim garments.

## Trends Shaping Textile & Apparel Sourcing

### Apparel: reading the market signal
Apparel exports in 2025 were lower than in 2023. A change in the sector total tells you nothing about one factory or one style, so use it as a signal of market mood and not as a price forecast. Ask each factory about current capacity, fabric lead times and seasonal bookings before you commit to a delivery date.

### Home textiles and technical fabrics
Home textile exports were lower than in 2023 and technical fabric exports were higher than in 2023. Home textile buyers should compare weight, size and finishing, because the same product name can hide very different quality. Technical fabric buyers should ask for test reports that name the test method and cover each batch.

### Compliance requests are part of the order
Retailers and brands commonly ask for chemical safety and traceability documents, for example OEKO-TEX test reports, or GOTS certificates when you make organic claims. Ask factories early which documents they already hold and which they need to obtain for your order. This prevents delays at listing and at customs.

## Turkey's Position for Buyers

- **Short distances to Europe.** Turkey borders Europe and the Middle East, so road and short sea routes are practical for many orders. Actual transit time depends on the origin city, the border crossing and the cargo, and your quotation states the expected time.
- **Customs union with the EU.** Industrial goods made in Turkey, including textiles, can enter the European Union under the EU-Turkey customs union, subject to rules of origin and the correct origin document. We check the documents case by case.
- **A connected supply chain.** Fabric, dyeing, printing and garment making are available in the same country. This helps you keep control over quality and lead time, and it gives you several suppliers to compare.
- **Flexible production sizes.** Many factories accept smaller runs than very large producers elsewhere, but minimums vary by fabric, colour and process. Your shortlist shows each factory's real minimum.
