### Calculation Notes

The total adds the product groups that carry no "of which" label. Each change is calculated as the 2025 value minus the 2023 value, divided by the 2023 value. Rows marked "of which" are shown for information. Their codes are already part of another row, so they are not added again.

### What the Codes Include

- **Extra Virgin Olive Oil.** Headings 1509 and 1510 cover olive oil and other oils obtained solely from olives, in bulk and in packs, including olive-pomace oil. They exclude table olives. Annual values reflect both the quantity sold and the price, and both can change from one year to the next.
- **Dried Fruits (Figs, Apricots, Raisins).** The three codes cover figs, raisins and dried apricots only. Other dried fruits, nuts and fruit mixes are reported separately, so the figure does not describe the whole dried fruit sector.
- **Hazelnuts & Nut Ingredients.** The two codes cover hazelnuts in shell and shelled hazelnuts. Roasted, sliced, ground and paste products fall under other headings, so the total shows raw and shelled supply rather than all hazelnut products.
- **Pasta, Pulses & Cereals.** Heading 1902 covers pasta and couscous, heading 0713 covers dried pulses and heading 1904 covers prepared cereal products, including bulgur. Flour, rice and other grain products are reported elsewhere, so the total covers only these three groups.

## Trends Shaping Food & Agriculture Sourcing

### Olive oil: harvest-driven values
Olive oil exports in 2025 were lower than in 2023. Annual export value reflects both the quantity sold and the price, and both change with the harvest. Ask for the crop year and for laboratory and sensory analysis of the batch you buy, and compare offers on the same grade.

### Dried fruits and hazelnuts: safety first
Dried fruit exports were higher than in 2023 and hazelnut exports were higher than in 2023. Both are natural products with strict contaminant limits in many markets. Plan laboratory tests, including aflatoxin, for each lot before shipment, and agree who pays for them.

### Pasta, pulses and cereals: private label and shelf life
Exports of pasta, pulses and cereal products were lower than in 2023. These are dry, easy to ship products, but labelling errors and moisture damage cause most problems. Approve label artwork in writing and fix production date and best-before requirements in the contract.

## Turkey's Position for Buyers

- **An agricultural base at home.** Olive groves, orchards, grain and pulse fields supply processors in several regions. Buyers can compare many producers for the same product group.
- **Food safety systems.** Many exporters hold certificates such as BRCGS, IFS, FSSC 22000 or ISO 22000. A certificate must cover the product and the site you buy from, so we ask to see its scope.
- **Bulk and retail-ready supply.** Producers offer bulk shipments for further processing as well as retail packs and private label packing, so you can choose where to add value.
- **Routes to nearby markets.** Road and sea transport serve Europe, the Middle East and Africa. Temperature and moisture protection matter, and we plan them with the carrier.
